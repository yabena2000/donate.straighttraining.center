Our story

It started with a handful of orphans from one street.

In 2014, when Modest turned 18, he wanted to give back. He had been lucky: a private school, university, a job as a tour guide – and fluent English. The children on his street in Fuoni had none of that. So he started teaching them English in the evenings.

Fast forward twelve years: Straight Training Center (formerly Straight Line) now teaches more than 200 students, most of them orphans or from families who struggle to get by. All seven teachers are former students. Besides the evening English class we offer computer classes and a weekend literacy class for parents who never learned to read and write.

Through debates, role plays, theatre and games our students practise English – and find a safe place where they belong, have role models and have fun. Many dream of becoming tour guides like Modest, a real way into work on an island that lives from tourism.

Why our own building

Rent has been our biggest problem for twelve years.

Modest pays the rent from his income as a tour guide. In the low season that income drops – and more than once the school had to close for a month or longer. Since our registration in 2022, taxes have added 60 % to our monthly costs. Our own building solves this for good.

No more rent

Every shilling that went to a landlord can go into teaching. The school becomes financially sustainable.

No more closures

In Zanzibar, tenants are poorly protected – landlords can take a building back at short notice. Our own walls mean classes never stop again.

Room to grow

Seven classrooms, a computer room, an office and an assembly hall – instead of children sitting on mats in a few rented rooms.

A home for volunteers

Volunteer rooms upstairs let teachers from abroad live at the school and teach – and build lasting friendships.

The building

Built in phases – every phase is a usable step.

After phase 1 the government allows us to teach on our own plot. Each further phase adds rooms. We buy all materials ourselves and bargain every price; Modest and his father, an engineer, supervise the construction.

  1. Phase0

    Permits & preparation

    Land transfer, building plans, contractor engineer – and the building permit, granted on 22 September 2026.

    We are allowed to build.

  2. Phase1a

    Outer wall, gate & foundation

    A wall around the plot, a temporary gate – and, earlier than first planned, the reinforced foundation for all ground-floor rooms with deep pillars for the roof.

    The groundwork is done: the building can rise.

  3. Phase1b

    The roof

    Concrete pillars and one large iron-sheet roof over the whole building.

    The site is protected before the heavy rains.

  4. Phase1c

    Classrooms 1–3, office, toilets, water & power

    The minimum the government requires to run a school on the site – plus the well, sewage tank and electricity. The power company only connects plots once rooms stand, and drilling the well needs power, so these come now.

    We move in. No more rent.

  5. Phase2

    Computer room, security room & stairs

    Classroom 4 becomes the computer room; a guard sleeps on site. Its foundation is already in place.

    Our 10 computers get a safe, permanent home.

  6. Phase3

    Upper floor: classrooms 5–7 & assembly hall

    Three more classrooms and an open hall for debates, theatre and events.

    Room for all 200+ students – and more.

  7. Phase4

    Volunteer rooms

    A kitchen, two bedrooms and two bathrooms.

    Volunteers can live at the school and teach.

  8. Phase5

    Garden

    Trees and a green courtyard.

    Shade and a place to play.

Costs per phase from our quotation, including a 20 % buffer for rising fuel and material prices. Building rarely goes exactly to plan: on the engineer's advice we laid the foundation for all rooms at once, and the wet ground needed far more rubble than expected, so the foundation cost more than budgeted. We update this page when the budget changes – every shilling is visible in the ledger.

Open books

Every shilling is booked in public.

We believe donors deserve to see exactly what happens with their money – and we want to show that a grassroots project in Africa can handle funds with complete integrity.

  • Every purchase – every bag of cement, every truck of sand, every fundi's wage – is entered in our public construction ledger, with photos of receipts.
  • Where the informal economy offers no receipt, we photograph the goods and document the payment ourselves.
  • The numbers on this page are read directly from that ledger. Nobody types them in by hand.
  • Large donors are named; small donations are grouped by channel (bank, crypto, mobile money).

Who receives your donation?

So that you can give with a cheap local transfer, Sustainable Integration GmbH, a company in Switzerland, receives donations for Straight Training Center in euros, Swiss francs, pounds and dollars, and via Revolut. Under a written agreement with the school, it collects them, converts them to euros once a month and forwards everything in a single transfer to the school's own account in Zanzibar.

Your donation goes into dedicated donation accounts named “Donations to Straight Training Center”. They are completely separate from the company's own money and are used for nothing else. The only deductions are the actual bank fees for currency conversion and the monthly transfer, plus about €7 the school's bank charges on arrival – shared across all donations of the month, so even small gifts arrive almost in full. Every donation, every fee and every monthly transfer is booked in our public ledger.

Please use exactly the reference shown above – usually “Donation to Straight Training Center” – so it is clear the money is passed on to the school. Because Sustainable Integration GmbH is a company and not a registered charity, donations through it are not tax-deductible.

Want a thank-you and to see your name in the ledger? Add your name after the reference – e.g. “Donation to Straight Training Center – Anna Meier” – or send us a short message. Otherwise your gift is booked anonymously under its channel.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Who owns the building?
Straight Training Center owns the plot and the building. In our agreement with Dein Stern für Afrika we committed to use it for education for at least ten years.
What happens if you raise more than needed?
Money is used for the next phase in order. Once the building is finished, extra funds go into running the school – rent no longer being one of them.
What if the building costs more than planned?
Material prices in Zanzibar change with fuel prices, so we included a 20 % buffer. The foundation still cost more than planned. When costs change, we update the goal on this page – and the reason is visible in the ledger.
Can I get a donation receipt for my taxes?
Not at the moment. Donations go either to the school in Tanzania or through Sustainable Integration GmbH, which is a company and not a charity – neither is tax-deductible in Europe. We are working on a tax-deductible option.
Can I donate anonymously?
Yes. Without a name in the reference, your donation is booked anonymously under its channel (bank, crypto, mobile money). We only name donors who want to be named.
Can I visit the school or volunteer?
Yes! Visitors are very welcome – please contact Modest in advance. Once the volunteer rooms are built, volunteers will be able to stay at the school.
Can I donate equipment instead of money?
Please contact us first so we can tell you what is needed most and how to get it to Zanzibar.
